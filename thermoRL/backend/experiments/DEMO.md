## Short demo script

1. **Question:** can placement use cooling feedback to reduce coolant extremes beyond what power balancing already achieves? Establish a credible testbed before interpreting policy outcomes.
2. **Built:** RAPS places modeled workload jobs on exact nodes; loss-inclusive rack power drives the Summit POWER9 FMU, whose published coolant values feed subsequent placement decisions. All six acceptance gates and full provenance apply, with explicitly scoped repeat evidence.
3. **Selection:** the preregistered three-FMU gate selected Summit. G1 froze the heuristic; the room-air lever missed its threshold, so G5/E13 was not run and no substitute lever was forced.
4. **Evaluation:** five policies, identical workloads/power/start times, synthetic then Alibaba-derived. One rule was fixed in advance: TP must lower mean max-rack coolant versus PB by at least 0.5 °C in every seed.
5. **Result:** **no material value on the homogeneous twin**. Largest PB−TP gaps were 0.34 °C for E11 and 0.18 °C for E12. Show a labelled within-experiment heatmap/GIF while retaining its scale and negative-outcome caption. E11 packing ≈ TP on the primary metric; E12 has the narrower descriptive ordering below. Neither supports a general scheduling benefit.
6. **Meaning and limits:** the testbed gives auditable negative answers within these regimes. Heterogeneous cooling, degraded cooling or other power-density regimes are future research questions requiring separate decisions, not results or planned execution in this demo.

> In E12 (small, low-density, bursty Alibaba-derived jobs), thermal_predictive had the lowest and packing the highest time-mean max-rack coolant temperature in all three seeds (PB−TP 0.08–0.18 °C; packing−TP 0.35–0.44 °C). This is below the preregistered 0.5 °C materiality threshold, rests on about two independent workload conditions (seed 3 ≈ seed 2 shifted 33 s), and is descriptive only.
